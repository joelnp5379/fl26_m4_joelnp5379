Answers clarify the requirements. They do not describe a design: how to meet a requirement is yours

Scope
Question Answer
Can I keep my M4 design of one
thread per build and add a
mutex?
No. R1 requires one build thread for every build, from the first
build until exit.
Must the worker exist from the
moment the application starts?
It must exist from the first build until exit. Creating it earlier is
fine.
Do I need a queue? No. R2 asks that at most one build runs and that an outdated
request is never built. Whether you hold requests in a queue or
some other way is your design, as long as R2 holds.
Is producer/consumer from the
Nov 17 lecture required?
No. Only Thread Synchronization (Nov 3) and the State pattern
(Nov 12) are required.
Is pair programming required in
M5?
No. It was part of M4 only.
Can I use std::jthread or
std::stop_token ?
No. The course uses C++17, and both are C++20.
Behavior
Question Answer
What does "about one
document's build time"
mean?
With --build-delay-ms 500 , roughly half a second plus normal
processing. The autograder allows a margin above one document's
time; a build that keeps running for several documents fails.
Does a remote add through
aiws_send count as a
Workspace change?
Yes (Section 4). It has the same effect on corpus state as Add
Document.
Must Build Corpus stay
disabled while a build runs?
That is decision 1 in Section 4. Either choice can be correct: your
states must handle whatever the user can do, and DESIGN.md
records your answer.
Is there one right answer to
the decisions in Section 4?
No. Each has more than one reasonable answer. Graders check
that your answer is clear and that your code does what you say it
does.
May the GUI thread wait for
the worker?
Not for a build (R1 in M4, R2 in M5). Joining the worker at exit is
expected (R4).

Question Answer
What happens to the result
of a cancelled build?
It never becomes current (R3). Whether it is produced at all is your
design.
State pattern
Question Answer
How many states do I need? As many as your application's corpus lifecycle needs. DESIGN.md
item 3 lists them; the State pattern review checks that the code
and DESIGN.md agree.
May a state object hold data? Yes. What a state holds is your design.
May I keep an enum, for
example for status text or
debugging?
Yes, for display or debugging. No transition may be decided by a
switch or if chain over it (constraint 1).
Must every state handle every
event?
Section 6 applies. How each state handles each event is your
design; DESIGN.md item 3 records it.
Deliverables
Question Answer
Do I still need
DESIGN_NOTE.md ?
No.
Do the M4 fault
switches still have
to work?
No. Only the two M5 macros are built and tested.
Does the one-page
limit include tables?
Yes, about 500 words in total. Keep the trace short: one line per step,
naming the function and the thread.
What should the
walkthrough video
show?
The three things in Section 9, in your own words, with your code and your
running application on screen. About a minute each is enough. Slides are
not needed.
How do I record and
submit it?
Use the course's Panopto: in Canvas, open Panopto from the course menu
and go to the [assignments] folder. Record with Panopto Capture (in
your browser, nothing to install) or use Upload media for a video you
recorded elsewhere (for example with Zoom or QuickTime). Only you and
course staff can see your video there. Then open your video, click Share,
and copy its link into VIDEO.md in your submission.
What if Panopto
does not work for
me?
Upload the video to OneDrive or Google Drive, share it so course staff can
view it, and put that link in VIDEO.md . Open the link in a private browser
window to check that it works.
May I read from
notes?
Glancing at your own notes is fine. What graders look for is you explaining,
in your own words, what is on the screen.

Question Answer
Do I have to rebuild
with the fault on
camera?
The difference between the normal and the faulted build must be visible in
the video. You may cut the wait for the rebuild out of the recording.
Why does the video
matter so much?
It is how M5 shows that the design is yours. Without it, the Synchronization
and State pattern reviews count at most half their points (Section 10).
Does the video
count for the on-
time bonus?
VIDEO.md is part of your Gradescope submission, so it follows the same rule:
its link must be in your last submission. Do not replace the video after you
submit.
How many videos do
I submit?
One, at most 3 minutes, covering the three points in Section 9.
What does
HISTORY.txt need to
show?
Commits in both weeks of M5, so the grader can see how your work
developed. A history with one commit near the deadline loses 0.5 points in
the DESIGN.md category.
Is AI use allowed? Yes, with the disclosure in DESIGN.md . The final exam asks about your own
design, so make sure you understand every part of what you submit.
What will the final
exam ask about M5?
Questions about the design you submitted: your states and the events that
change them, how your worker waits, is cancelled, and stops, and what
your code does in a given sequence of events. Answers are compared with
your code. You do not need to memorize code; you need to know your
design. These questions count toward the final exam grade, not toward M5.
